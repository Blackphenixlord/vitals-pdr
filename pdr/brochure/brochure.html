An onboard medical assistant for long-duration Moon and Mars missions

The problem

No doctor on board.
Help is up to 40 minutes away.

On Mars, a message to the flight surgeon takes 4 to 40 minutes each way, and NASA plans for blackouts of up to two weeks. Most crews will have no physician — at best a crewmember with paramedic training. When someone gets sick or hurt, they have to answer three questions fast:

What could this be?
How serious is it — do we call the ground now?
What do we do in the meantime?
VITALS is an onboard medical assistant a non-medical crewmember can talk to in plain words. It runs with no internet, it knows spaceflight medicine, it puts urgency first — and it shows the source behind every answer.

Earth symptom checkers assume Earth. In space, a stuffy nose on day 2 is fluid shift, not a cold — and joint pain after a spacewalk is decompression sickness until proven otherwise.
