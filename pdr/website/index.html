VITALS

When the doctor is 20 minutes away, the crew still needs an answer now. VITALS is an onboard medical assistant a non-medical crewmember can talk to — offline, grounded in spaceflight research, and honest about what it doesn't know.

The problem

On Mars, a message to the flight surgeon takes 4 to 40 minutes each way, and NASA plans for blackouts of up to two weeks. Most crews will have no physician on board. When someone gets sick or hurt, the person helping them needs to know: what could this be, how serious is it, and what do we do now?

What it does

Talk in your own words

“Bad pain in my right side, comes in waves, blood in my urine.” No medical vocabulary needed.

Urgency first

The escalation level is the first thing on screen — as a word and a color, never color alone.

Every answer has a source

It can only name conditions we researched, and it never writes its own citations — they come from our knowledge base.

Safety backstop

A rules engine checks every answer. If it says escalate, the answer escalates, whatever the AI said.

Fully offline

Runs on an NVIDIA Jetson in the habitat with the language model built in. No internet, ever.

A doctor in the loop (planned)

Ground crew will send a redacted case to a doctor and check the reply before the crew sees it.

How it works

crew message→ is it medical?→ read the findings→ pick cited KB pages→ local AI reasons over those pages only→ check the reply + rules backstop→ answer + sources

The astronaut screen works today. Planned: a ground crew screen (sees everything, decides what the doctor sees) and a doctor screen (sees only the redacted case), all running from the one Jetson.
